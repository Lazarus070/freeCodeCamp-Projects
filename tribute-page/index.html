Targeted Molecular Oncology

Conventional chemotherapy circulates throughout the entire human body, damaging rapidly dividing healthy tissue alongside malignant tumors. Prof. Tebello Nyokong dedicated her career at Rhodes University in Makhanda to solving this precision problem through photodynamic therapy (PDT) and medicinal nanotechnology.

By synthesizing custom phthalocyanines—deep blue-green synthetic dye molecules chemically related to hemoglobin and chlorophyll—and coupling them with metallic nanoparticles, her laboratory engineered photosensitizers that remain inert in the dark. Once accumulated inside cancerous tissue and illuminated by a non-thermal red laser, the molecules trigger a localized photochemical reaction that eliminates malignant cells with minimal collateral damage.

Milestones & Scientific Legacy

  1. 1951

    Early Life in Lesotho

    Born on October 20, 1951. Attended school on alternating days while herding sheep, developing an early determination to pursue science despite limited rural resources.

  2. 1977

    BSc in Chemistry & Biology

    Graduated from the National University of Lesotho before earning a scholarship to pursue graduate studies in Canada.

  3. 1987

    PhD in Chemistry

    Completed her doctorate at the University of Western Ontario, specializing in the electrochemistry and photophysics of phthalocyanines.

  4. 2004

    Order of Mapungubwe

    Awarded South Africa’s highest national honor for achievements in science, recognizing her transformative contributions to medicinal chemistry.

  5. 2007

    DST/Mintek Nanotechnology Innovation Centre

    Appointed Director of the national Nanotechnology Innovation Centre at Rhodes University, mentoring dozens of postgraduate African scientists.

  6. 2009

    L’Oréal-UNESCO Laureate for Women in Science

    Named the African & Arab States Laureate for pioneering work on harnessing light to treat cancer and remediate environmental pollutants.
